# Contract: The grading table

**Externally imposed, reproduced verbatim.** This is the only file in the repository that EPYHIA
does not get to decide the contents of. It is the source of truth for the `area` and `points`
fields of [`eval/rubric.json`](./eval-rubric.schema.json) (`DESIGN.md` §10.2, FR-067).

It exists because the alternative is worse: without it, `rubric.json` is authored from a
document living outside the repository, and nothing — no schema, no test, no reviewer — can tell
whether the rubric covers every scored area or whether its points add up. Tracking the table
turns "the totals add to 100" from a claim into a check.

**Do not edit this file to match an implementation.** If `rubric.json` disagrees with it,
`rubric.json` is the thing to fix. If the external table itself changes, update this file in one
commit that changes nothing else, so the diff is legible as "the contract moved" rather than
"we scored ourselves differently".

## 1 · The table

> **7 · Grading (100 pts) & how you prove it**
>
> A **measurable rubric** + a **video demo**, consistent with the track. Ship an `eval/` (a
> `rubric.json` and an `eval.py` you write) that checks the automated criteria against your
> running agency and writes `PRODUCT_EVAL.md` — your submission.

| Area | Pts | What earns them |
|---|---|---|
| The three deliverables are **real** | 30 | Live URL, on-brand grounded pack, a test purchase that persists an order |
| Not slop | 15 | The site reads like a real brand's, not an AI template |
| The crew & orchestration | 15 | Strategist delegates only; scoped agents; brand doc changes behavior; cost logged |
| The Action Gate | 20 | Single door for spend/publish; only credential holder; approval before irreversible; idempotent; audit + cost |
| Design & failure catalogue (`DESIGN.md` first) | 10 | Argued choices; 5 real failure modes each with a control |
| Ships & runs from clean clone | 10 | Deployed agency URL; `.env.example`; one command up; the demo |

> **The two rows that carry it:** the checkout **actually persists a real order** (no fake
> success screen), and a **re-run produces no duplicate site or charge**. Those are the line
> between an agency you'd trust with a credit card and one you wouldn't.

## 2 · Area ids

`rubric.json` references areas by id, not by prose title, so that a wording change in the table
above does not silently orphan every entry. The schema constrains `area` to exactly this set.

| Id | Area | Points |
|---|---|---|
| `deliverables-real` | The three deliverables are real | 30 |
| `not-slop` | Not slop | 15 |
| `crew-orchestration` | The crew & orchestration | 15 |
| `action-gate` | The Action Gate | 20 |
| `design-failure-catalogue` | Design & failure catalogue | 10 |

## 3 · What is checked against this file

Mechanically, by a test — not by reading (FR-067):

| Assertion | Why |
|---|---|
| Every `area` in `rubric.json` is one of the six ids above | A typo would otherwise create a seventh area worth nothing |
| Every one of the six ids appears in `rubric.json` at least once | An omitted area is the failure this file exists to catch — three of the six are entirely human-judged and are the easiest to forget |
| The `points` of all entries in an area sum to that area's points | Per-area, not just in total, so a surplus in one area cannot mask a deficit in another |

Nothing here dictates *how many* entries an area carries, or which are `automated` versus
`evidence`. That is `rubric.json`'s judgement and is bounded by FR-063: an area that cannot be
mechanically checked carries `evidence` rows which resolve to links and award themselves
nothing.
